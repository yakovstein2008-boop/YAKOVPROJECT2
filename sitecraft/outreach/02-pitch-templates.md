# 02 — The Pitch Templates (your "kickers")

Six templates, each built on a proven copywriting framework and tuned to Sitecraft's
unfair advantage: **you can hand someone a finished preview, risk-free.** Below each one
is *why it works* and *when to use it*, then a subject-line bank and a 4-email follow-up
sequence that does most of the heavy lifting.

## How to use this file

1. **Pick the template** that matches the prospect's situation (each says who it's for).
2. **Fill the `{{variables}}`** — see the personalization guide at the bottom. The one that
   matters most is `{{specific_observation}}`: one true, specific detail about *them*.
3. **Keep it short, plain-text, one CTA.** The CTA is almost always "want me to send the
   preview?" — the lowest-friction, highest-value ask you have.
4. **Always follow up** (Day 3 / 10 / 17). Over half of all replies come from follow-ups.

### The variables (quick legend)
`{{FirstName}}` · `{{Business}}` · `{{trade}}` (plumbing, lawn care…) · `{{City}}` ·
`{{specific_observation}}` (the custom detail) · `{{Name}}` (you) · footer from `01`.

## ⭐ Template 1 — "I already made you something"
**Framework:** Pattern-interrupt / give-value-first. **Best for:** Tier A (no website or
Facebook-only). **This is your strongest opener — start here.**

> **Subject:** made you something, {{FirstName}}
>
> Hi {{FirstName}} —
>
> I build websites for {{trade}} businesses around {{City}}, and {{specific_observation}}, so I
> went ahead and started a free preview of a site for {{Business}}. No catch — I just figured
> you'd land more jobs with it than without.
>
> Want me to send the link? If you don't love it, no hard feelings and we never speak of it again.
>
> {{Name}}
>
> P.S. The way I work: you only pay if you actually love it — $450 flat, due after it's live. The
> preview is free either way.

**Why it works:** It leads with *given value*, not an ask — the single biggest pattern-interrupt
in cold email. "Want me to send the link?" is almost impossible to say no to (tiny ask, concrete
payoff). The risk-reversal P.S. removes the only real objection. Plain-text, ~80 words, one CTA.

---

## Template 2 — PAS (Problem · Agitate · Solve)
**Framework:** PAS — leads with pain, best for first-touch cold. **Best for:** word-of-mouth
businesses with weak or no online presence.

> **Subject:** when people search "{{trade}} near me"
>
> Hi {{FirstName}} —
>
> Every week people near {{City}} search "{{trade}} near me" and call whoever shows up first.
> Right now, for a lot of those searches, {{Business}} is invisible — so that job goes to a
> competitor, even when your work is better.
>
> I fix exactly that for local {{trade}} businesses. I'll build you a free preview of a site
> that shows up and turns visitors into phone calls — you only pay ($450 flat) if you love it.
>
> Want me to send it over?
>
> {{Name}}

**Why it works:** Names a real, vivid pain (lost jobs to competitors), twists it gently
("even when your work is better"), then resolves with a no-risk offer. PAS is the highest-
performing structure for a *first* cold email because it creates urgency before you pitch.

---

## Template 3 — BAB (Before · After · Bridge)
**Framework:** BAB — paints the after-state. **Best for:** Facebook/Instagram-only businesses.

> **Subject:** more than a Facebook page
>
> Hi {{FirstName}} —
>
> A Facebook page is a great start, but when someone wants to *hire* {{Business}}, they often
> can't find hours, services, or a quick way to book — so they bounce.
>
> Picture a clean site that comes up when they Google you, shows your reviews, and lets them
> call or book in one tap. That's the whole game for a {{trade}} business.
>
> I'll build {{Business}} a free preview so you can see it for real. Pay only if you love it —
> $450 flat. Want the link?
>
> {{Name}}

**Why it works:** Validates what they already have (no insult), then makes the better future
concrete and desirable, then bridges with the free preview. BAB shines when the gap between
"now" and "better" is easy to picture — which is exactly the case for a FB-only business.

## Template 4 — "3 quick wins" (the redesign angle)
**Framework:** Specific-ideas / consultative. **Best for:** Tier B (has a dated or basic site).

> **Subject:** 3 quick wins for {{Business}}'s site
>
> Hi {{FirstName}} —
>
> I had a look at {{Business}}'s site and there are a few quick wins that'd likely get you more
> calls:
>
> 1. Click-to-call in the header (so a phone visitor dials in one tap)
> 2. Your Google reviews shown up front (social proof sells before you pick up)
> 3. A short "request a visit" form that lands jobs in your inbox
>
> I can show you exactly how it'd look — I'll build a free preview of a refreshed version. Only
> pay ($450 flat) if it beats what you've got. Want me to send it?
>
> {{Name}}

**Why it works:** Three specific, *positive* suggestions prove you actually looked and frame you
as helpful, not critical. Then the same free-preview CTA. Consultative > salesy for owners who
already invested in a site.

---

## Template 5 — The one-liner (ultra-short)
**Framework:** Minimal-friction. **Best for:** busy owners; great as a fast variant of any of the above.

> **Subject:** quick one
>
> Hi {{FirstName}} — I make websites for {{trade}} businesses in {{City}}. I'll build {{Business}}
> a free preview, and you only pay ($450 flat) if you love it. Want me to send the link?
>
> {{Name}}

**Why it works:** Some of the highest reply rates come from emails a prospect can read in five
seconds on their phone. Nothing to object to, one tiny ask. Use it when you have little to
personalize, or as the Day-3 follow-up to a longer first email.

---

## Template 6 — "Congrats on the new business" (lock it in)
**Framework:** Trigger-event / timeliness. **Best for:** newly opened businesses (a real
"trigger" — these convert 3–5× better than generic outreach).

> **Subject:** congrats on {{Business}}
>
> Hi {{FirstName}} —
>
> Saw {{Business}} is up and running — congrats, the early days are a grind. One thing that pays
> off fast: a real website so the customers Googling "{{trade}} near me" find *you* and not just
> the big franchises.
>
> I build these for local {{trade}} businesses — I'll do a free preview for {{Business}} and you
> only pay ($450 flat) if you love it. Want me to send it over?
>
> {{Name}}
>
> P.S. New businesses often add a companion app (+$249) so repeat customers have you one tap away
> on their home screen — happy to include that in the preview if it's useful.

**Why it works:** Acting on a trigger event (a new opening) is the single biggest reply-rate
multiplier in cold email. The congratulations is genuine and disarming; the app P.S. plants an
upsell without diluting the main CTA.

# Subject-line bank

The data is consistent: **2–4 words, lowercase, curiosity- or question-based, personalized**
win. Personalized subjects get ~46% open vs ~35% without. **Keep "free" OUT of the subject**
(spam trigger) — put it in the body. Rotate these:

**Curiosity / pattern-interrupt**
- made you something, {{FirstName}}
- a preview for {{Business}}
- {{Business}} — built you something
- quick one
- this took me 20 minutes

**Question-based** (top open-rate format)
- {{Business}} on Google?
- worth a look?
- are you the right person?
- still taking new customers?
- mind if I send this over?

**Trigger / specific**
- when people search "{{trade}} near me"
- saw your reviews, {{FirstName}}
- congrats on {{Business}}
- noticed {{Business}} isn't on Google yet

**Plain & personal** (highest single open rate in studies: "Hi {{FirstName}}")
- Hi {{FirstName}}
- {{FirstName}} — quick idea
- for {{Business}}

> Tip: write the body first, then pull the subject from the most curious line in it. Never let
> the subject "give away the ending."

# The follow-up sequence (where the money is)

First email ≈ 37% of replies. Follow-ups ≈ the other 55%+. Send **4 touches total** on a
widening **3 / 7 / 7** cadence (Day 0, 3, 10, 17), each adding something new. Reply in the
**same thread** (keeps subject, builds context). Stop the moment they reply or say stop.

### Touch 1 — Day 0
Your chosen template above (start with Template 1 or 6).

### Touch 2 — Day 3 — *the highest-leverage follow-up*
> Hi {{FirstName}} — floating this back up in case it got buried. The free preview offer stands:
> I'll build {{Business}} a site, you only pay if you love it. Want me to send the link?
>
> {{Name}}

*Day 3 catches everyone who saw the first one and meant to reply. Keep it to two lines.*

### Touch 3 — Day 10 — add proof / a new angle
> Hi {{FirstName}} — quick example of why I'm pushing on this: a local service site with
> click-to-call and reviews up front routinely turns more visitors into actual phone calls than
> a Facebook page or no site at all. I'd love to show you what that looks like for {{Business}} —
> free preview, pay only if you love it. Worth a look?
>
> {{Name}}

### Touch 4 — Day 17 — the "breakup" (these convert surprisingly well)
> Hi {{FirstName}} — I'll stop here so I'm not a pain. If a website for {{Business}} is ever on
> your list, just reply and I'll build you a free preview, no strings. Either way, wishing you a
> ton of business. 👍
>
> {{Name}}

*The breakup email triggers loss-aversion ("last chance") and reads as respectful, not pushy —
it's consistently one of the top-replying messages in a sequence.*

# Personalization — the 60-second rule

The framework gives you structure; **personalization gives you the reply.** Personalized emails
get ~21–26% higher response. Before each send, spend 30–60 seconds finding ONE true, specific
detail and drop it into `{{specific_observation}}`. Where to look and example fills:

| Where you look | Example `{{specific_observation}}` |
|----------------|-------------------------------------|
| They have no website (Google Maps shows no link) | "I noticed {{Business}} doesn't have a website yet" |
| Facebook-only | "I found {{Business}} on Facebook but couldn't find a website" |
| A great recent review | "your reviews are genuinely great — people mention {{detail}}" |
| A service they highlight | "I saw you specialize in {{service}}" |
| Years in business / family-owned | "I saw you've been family-run since {{year}}" |
| A new opening | "I saw {{Business}} just opened" |
| Dated site | "your site looks like it hasn't been touched in a while" |

**Do NOT** fake personalization or use a detail that could apply to anyone — that reads worse
than no personalization. One real specific beats three generic compliments.
